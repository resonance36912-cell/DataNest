# Resonance Certification & Assurance — Commercial Service Terms Framework

**Status:** Business launch framework; legal review should precede adoption as a binding customer contract  
**Service standard:** Resonance Certification Standard (RCS) 1.0  
**Provider platform:** Resonance DataNest

## 1. Service relationship

Resonance Certification & Assurance provides scoped readiness, assurance, certification, standards-alignment, surveillance and evidence-pack services through the DataNest governance and evidence control plane.

Each engagement is defined by a service request, target, scope, criteria, evidence basis, deliverables and responsible human reviewers.

## 2. Scope control

The customer and Resonance define the target and assessment scope before formal assessment work. Material scope changes may require a revised assessment, quotation, timeline or certification decision.

A certificate applies only to its stated scope and validity boundary.

## 4. Deliverables

Deliverables are the records stated in the selected RCS service offering. They may include readiness reports, applicability profiles, evidence plans, remediation registers, assessment findings, review records, certification decisions, certificates, evidence references and surveillance outcomes.

## 5. Customer responsibilities

The customer is responsible for providing accurate scope information, lawful access to required evidence, suitable contacts, timely responses, and notice of material changes that could affect the certification basis.

Resonance may record missing, contradictory or stale evidence rather than treating it as satisfied.

## 6. Certification decisions

RCS certification is an evidence-led human decision. No customer payment, request, marketing relationship or prior certification guarantees issuance.

Issuance requires the controls specified by RCS, including a defined scope, approved standards profile, captured evidence, resolved blocking findings, closed assessment and authorized human decision.

## 7. External standards boundary

Where external standards are used as criteria, the engagement records their applicability and evidence basis.

RCS does not represent an internal Resonance certificate as ISO certification, ISO endorsement, accreditation, regulatory approval or third-party conformity certification. ISO states that it does not itself perform certification or issue certificates; certification is performed by external certification bodies. citeturn101218search0turn101218search6

Where a customer requires accredited certification to an external standard, the engagement should distinguish that requirement and direct the customer to an appropriately independent certification body.

## 8. Certification validity

Certificates are time-bounded. Material scope, operating-environment or evidence changes may require surveillance or re-assessment. Renewal is a new governed decision rather than an automatic extension.

A certificate may be suspended, revoked or expire when the governing conditions require it.

## 9. Evidence and confidentiality

Assessment evidence is handled according to the applicable DataNest visibility, privacy, retention and access controls. Public disclosure is limited to authorized references and claim metadata.

## 10. No automatic authority transfer

Certification does not create equity, ownership, royalty, debt, voting power, administrative authority, legal representation rights or production/deployment authority.

Certification evidence does not override DataNest authorization, security, privacy, legal or contractual controls.

## 11. Corrections and disputes

Certification records remain attributable and versioned. Corrections create new records or status events rather than silently rewriting historical evidence.

Customer questions, disputes or requests for correction should identify the engagement, certificate number or assessment reference so the governed record can be reviewed.

## 12. Governing legal documents

This framework is a commercial operating baseline, not a substitute for a customer-specific statement of work, terms of service, privacy notice, data-processing agreement, or jurisdiction-specific legal review where those are required.
