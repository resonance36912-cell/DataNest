# Resonance Certification Standard (RCS)

**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective:** 1 October 2026  
**Authority:** Resonance DataNest  
**Status:** Internal governed certification and assurance standard

## 1. Purpose

The Resonance Certification Standard (RCS) defines how Resonance may assess, certify, publish, monitor, suspend, revoke, and renew claims about a product, service, project, organization-controlled environment, or other defined scope.

RCS is an internal service and assurance standard. It is evidence-led, versioned, attributable, and explicitly bounded. A Resonance certificate does **not** by itself represent ISO certification, accreditation, regulatory approval, legal advice, or third-party conformity certification.

## 2. Core principle

**No certification claim without defined scope, applicable criteria, traceable evidence, review, and an attributable certification decision.**

Certification distinguishes what was actually assessed from what was not assessed. Unknown, missing, contradictory, or stale evidence cannot be silently converted into conformity.

## 3. Certification domains

RCS assessments may cover one or more of the following domains:

1. **Governance & authority** — identity, accountability, permissions, separation of powers, human oversight, and decision traceability.
2. **Evidence & provenance** — source identity, versioning, hashes, lineage, reproducibility, and evidence retention.
3. **Security & privacy** — security controls, access boundaries, disclosure controls, data minimization, and incident posture.
4. **Product & software quality** — functionality, reliability, quality-in-use, accessibility, usability, and release discipline.
5. **AI governance** — AI system purpose, authority boundaries, data and memory controls, evaluation, human oversight, and traceability.
6. **Operational & release assurance** — deployment controls, change management, monitoring, recovery, and post-release verification.
7. **Transparency & market integrity** — truthful service/product descriptions, claim boundaries, evidence availability, commercial-state disclosure, and separation of reputation from authority or financial rights.
8. **Standards alignment** — documented applicability and evidence against selected external standards or frameworks where relevant.

## 4. Certification classes

The standard supports these initial service certifications:

- **RCS-GOV-01 Governance & Control Assurance**
- **RCS-PROD-01 Product & Software Assurance**
- **RCS-AI-01 AI Governance Assurance**
- **RCS-DATA-01 Data & Privacy Assurance**
- **RCS-OPS-01 Operational & Release Assurance**
- **RCS-MKT-01 Transparency & Market Integrity Assurance**

A combined certification may cover multiple classes when the assessment scope and evidence support the combined decision.

## 5. Service lifecycle

The controlled service lifecycle is:

    enquiry
      -> intake
      -> scope definition
      -> applicability / standards profile
      -> evidence acquisition
      -> assessment
      -> findings
      -> remediation
      -> review
      -> certification decision
      -> certificate / evidence pack
      -> surveillance
      -> renewal, suspension, revocation, or expiry

Each stage is attributable to a project, assessment, revision, evidence set, and actor.

## 6. Minimum certification decision requirements

An RCS certificate may be issued only when:

- the assessment has a defined target and scope;
- the applicable standards profile has been approved;
- required evidence has been captured and remains traceable to the assessment revision;
- findings have been reviewed and unresolved draft, potential-gap, or verified-nonconformity states do not block the requested certification;
- the certification decision is made by an authorized human reviewer under the DataNest authority model;
- the certificate identifies its scope, issue date, validity boundary, criteria/version, and claim limitations.

No automated agent may self-issue, self-approve, extend, or revoke a certificate.

## 7. Certificate states

Certificates use these lifecycle states:

- **draft** — prepared but not issued;
- **issued** — certification decision completed and certificate active;
- **suspended** — temporarily not valid for the stated certification claim;
- **revoked** — certification claim withdrawn;
- **expired** — validity period ended without renewal.

Status changes are attributable events and must preserve prior evidence.

## 8. Validity and surveillance

A certificate is time-bounded. The validity period is defined at issuance according to scope and risk.

Surveillance or re-assessment may be required when:

- material scope changes occur;
- major controls change;
- the product or service enters a materially different operating environment;
- significant incidents affect the certification basis;
- evidence becomes stale;
- the certification period reaches its review point.

Renewal is a new governed decision; it is not an automatic extension.

## 9. External standards and accreditation boundary

External standards may be selected as assessment criteria through the existing standards register and applicability profile.

A certification record must distinguish:

- **Resonance internal certification** — issued under RCS;
- **standards alignment assessment** — documented comparison against selected criteria;
- **third-party / accredited certification** — issued by a separate authorized external conformity-assessment body.

Resonance must not represent an internal RCS certificate as an accredited certificate from ISO or another external body.

## 10. Business and market service offering

Certification is offered as a governed professional service capability within DataNest.

Initial service offerings:

### Certification Readiness Assessment
A scoped gap and evidence-readiness review that identifies prerequisites for an RCS certification decision. Output: readiness report, evidence plan, standards applicability profile, and remediation register.

### Resonance Certification Assessment
A formal evidence-led assessment against one or more RCS certification classes. Output: assessment record, findings/actions, review record, and certification decision.

### Certification & Evidence Pack
A governed certificate plus machine-readable and human-readable evidence references, scope, criteria/version, limitations, issue date, and validity boundary.

### Surveillance & Renewal
Periodic review of the certification basis, updated evidence, material changes, open findings, and renewal decision.

### Standards Alignment Review
A documented applicability and evidence assessment against selected external standards. It is an alignment service and does not itself imply external accreditation.

Service descriptions, certification status, and claim limitations must remain synchronized with the governed evidence record.

## 11. Commercial governance

The current market posture remains:

- free promotion is active;
- paid checkout is disabled;
- no pricing is implied by the service catalog;
- future pricing requires a separate governed commercial decision;
- no certificate may be sold as a shortcut around evidence or review requirements.

## 12. Market claims and disclosure

All public-facing certification claims must identify, where relevant:

- certification class;
- assessed scope;
- standard/RCS version;
- issue and expiry/review dates;
- certification state;
- evidence reference;
- material limitations;
- whether the result is internal Resonance certification, standards alignment, or third-party certification.

Popularity, customer count, testimonials, contribution, reputation, or internal utility credits do not constitute certification evidence.

## 13. Revocation and correction

A certificate may be suspended or revoked when its basis is materially invalidated, scope is misrepresented, required evidence is withdrawn, or a governed review determines that the claim can no longer stand.

Corrections preserve the original record and create a new attributable version; historical certification evidence is not silently rewritten.

## 14. Authority boundary

RCS does not authorize:

- production deployment;
- legal conclusions;
- regulatory approval;
- ownership transfer;
- equity, royalty, debt, or financial rights;
- security exceptions;
- bypass of DataNest governance;
- autonomous agent authority.

Certification describes the state supported by evidence at a defined time and scope.

## 15. Records and traceability

The certification system should retain:

- assessment identity and revision;
- approved standards profile;
- evidence sources and hashes;
- findings and actions;
- reviewer/certification decision identity;
- certificate identifier and state history;
- issue/expiry/review timestamps;
- public disclosure references where publication is authorized.

This record connects the market-facing claim to the underlying governed evidence.
